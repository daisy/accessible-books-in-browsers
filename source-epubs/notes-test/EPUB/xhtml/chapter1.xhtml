Chapter 1. Footnotes on the same page

The lighthouse keeper climbed the stairs every evening at dusk.1 She counted the steps as she went, out of habit rather than need.

1. There were one hundred and twelve steps, according to the keeper’s own notebook.

The lamp was lit by hand until the island was connected to the mainland grid.2 After that, the keeper’s work was mostly cleaning glass and keeping records.

2. This reference is a plain link with no note semantics, to test notes that are only recognized by their target.

On clear nights the beam could be seen from the far side of the bay. On foggy nights, the horn did the work instead.

Visitors were rare, and most of them came for the view.3 The keeper offered them tea and a short tour, and then sent them on their way before dark.

3. The view from the gallery takes in three islands and, on a good day, the mountains to the north.

Her notebook recorded the weather,4 the ships that passed,5 and the birds that rested on the gallery rail.6

4. Weather was noted three times a day: at dawn, at noon, and at dusk.

5. Ships were listed by name when the name could be read, and by flag when it could not.

6. Gulls were too common to count; the keeper only wrote down the unusual ones.
